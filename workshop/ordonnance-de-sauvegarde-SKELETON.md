## Governing framework (general, not case-specific)

### Vehicle

In Quebec family matters, interim access relief between appearances is
typically sought by one of:

- **Ordonnance de sauvegarde** — short-term protective order pending the
  next meaningful hearing, grounded in `art. 49 C.p.c.` (general power) and
  `art. 158 al. 1(5) C.p.c.` (case-management powers), with the
  family-specific hook in `art. 603 C.p.c.`
- **Demande en modification des mesures provisoires** — if there are
  already provisional measures in place and circumstances have changed.
- **Demande pour précisions / pour outrage** — if an existing order is
  being breached rather than needing modification.

The correct vehicle is **entirely determined by what the presiding judge
actually ordered in the most recent ruling**. Without the ruling text, the
vehicle cannot be chosen honestly. This skeleton assumes
`ordonnance de sauvegarde` because that is what the workshop prompt named;
substitute as needed once counsel reviews the ruling.

### Test for a safeguard order

Quebec jurisprudence generally requires the applicant to establish:

1. **Urgence** — a time-sensitive situation that cannot await the ordinary
   hearing calendar.
2. **Apparence de droit** — an arguable prima facie case on the merits.
3. **Préjudice sérieux ou irréparable** — harm that damages cannot
   adequately remedy.
4. **Balance des inconvénients (prépondérance des inconvénients)** — the
   relative hardship favours the applicant.

Commonly-cited anchors (all `[VÉRIFIER CITATION]` — do not rely on any of
these without pulling the actual decision):

- `[VÉRIFIER] RJR-MacDonald Inc. c. Canada (P.G.), [1994] 1 RCS 311` —
  three-part injunction test.
- `[VÉRIFIER] Droit de la famille — 091768, 2009 QCCA 1378` — safeguard
  criteria in family context.
- `[VÉRIFIER] Droit de la famille — 103038, 2010 QCCS XXXX` — short
  duration / urgency framing.
- `[VÉRIFIER] Droit de la famille — 20XXX, 20XX QCCA XXXX` — best
  interests of the child as overarching standard under `art. 33 C.c.Q.`

## Framing notes (register, not content)

Rhetorical registers that tend to land with a Superior Court bench, as
general drafting craft:

- **Documented deprivation of contact** over specific days, each tied to
  a pièce.
- **Pattern, not episode** — if a serial chronology exists, the facts
  themselves carry the rhetorical weight. No adjectives required.
- **Child-centred framing** — `art. 33 C.c.Q.` puts best-interests at the
  top; arguments phrased as the child's loss, not the parent's grievance,
  track that standard.
- **Restraint beats volume** — motions that read angry tend to lose even
  when the applicant is right. Dated, exhibited facts are the argument.
- Rhetorical frames like "psychological torture," even where lived
  experience supports them, will generally land poorly with a Quebec
  Superior Court judge and can undermine credibility.

Actual rhetorical passages must be drafted only once the pièces and the
governing ruling are in hand. Framing without facts is performance.
